## 03 · Marquee Hero

The hero is the page above the fold. A single statement or visual fills the viewport. Below the fold the page becomes something else (a list, a grid, prose).

- **Heading:** display fills the fold at `clamp(4rem, 10vw, 9rem)` (`craft.md` § 1), hugging the container's edges; an uppercase head that can wrap keeps `line-height` 1.0 or more (55a).
- **Body:** below the fold, a list of work or one content block; the hero does not continue.
- **Divider:** a thick rule between hero and below-fold, or a hard change of paper.
- **Button:** one action in the fold under the statement, with the one sentence (Floor 12, 13): a typographic link or a single button. The statement owns the screen; the action and the sentence sit small at its foot.
- **Image:** none in the fold (typography is the visual), or a single full-bleed photograph as the fold's background.
- **Reveal:** the fold is static; below-fold may sweep in horizontally.

Reach for it when the brand or the person is the message: designer and director portfolios, indie products with one declarative voice, any "this is who we are" page.

Avoid for products whose value needs explaining in seconds. Marquee makes the visitor scroll before they understand.

Compositions: a Poster or Corridor hero (`layout.md` § 6); below it, Rows, Grid or a Showcase (§ 5).

**Sample opening lines** (imitate the specificity, not the wording):
> *"Type, set with care."* Three words, refusal of the verb, treats type as material.
> *"A studio for what comes next."* Names the practice without explaining it.
> *"Print discipline, on screen."* Two phrases, a promise about method.
