## 07 · Manifesto

Polemical large type. The page tells the reader what to believe before it tells them what to buy. Often a poster's posture.

- **Heading:** uppercase display, tilted slightly (`-2deg` to `-4deg`) or set straight; one weight and one colour throughout, stress from a line break, never from a coloured or italic word (38i, 38iii); `line-height` 1.0 or more when it can wrap (55a). A theme whose signature is an accented word stamps the 38iii waiver and places it once, in the hero.
- **Body:** short paragraphs, each one assertion, large enough to read across the room.
- **Divider:** changes of paper between sections, or the theme's own rules; never a card.
- **Button:** one solid block in the accent; the fold carries it beside or under the declaration (44b), and it returns at the foot as the page's last word.
- **Image:** none when the cause is an idea; when it is physical (repair, housing, a river), photographs of it in the theme's treatment, one a screen at most.
- **Reveal:** one horizontal sweep, on the first claim below the fold; the rest is simply there.

Reach for it for repositioning announcements, mission-led indie products, a studio's beliefs page.

Avoid for checkout, pricing or a product tour. Manifesto sells agreement; a call to show up, sign or join is its one action.

```html
<section class="manifesto bleed-ink">
  <h1 class="caps display-xxl">We believe<br>design is slow.</h1>
</section>
<section class="bleed-paper">
  <p class="claim">It costs less than the rework you will do without it.</p>
</section>
```

**Sample opening lines** (manifestos commit; they do not hedge):
> *"We are a studio. We are not a platform."* Defines by refusal, one colour (Meridian).
> *"We design products that last twelve years. We do not design products that need replacing every two."* A concrete number, a paired declaration.
> *"Lightness above weight."* A one-line principle, no explanation.
