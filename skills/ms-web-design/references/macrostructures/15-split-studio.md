## 15 · Split Studio

Diptych. Every major block divides the screen, text on one side, proof on the other, and the proof keeps its side down the page (one mirror at most, never a zigzag, `layout.md` § 7).

- **Heading:** half the screen wide; the other half holds a capture or a supplied quote.
- **Body:** text-and-proof rows that vary by weight, not by side: one lead row gives the proof the larger share, the rest split evenly; one column gap for all of them (F15).
- **Divider:** a clear gutter between halves; no rules.
- **Button:** an outlined chip below the text half.
- **Image:** on the proof side in every row: a real capture, or the product built in a slot (`imagery.md` § 4).
- **Reveal:** the hero's two halves fade in once, 60 to 100ms apart; the rows below are simply there.

Reach for it for SaaS feature pages, developer tools that pair explanation with code, anything where every claim wants a visual proof.

Avoid for narrative or photographic brands. Split halves the attention; some pages need one focus.

Compositions: Split (`layout.md` § 5), the proof keeping its side.

**Sample opening lines** (a positioning statement paired with a proof column):
> *"A studio for what comes next."* Italic display beside a selected-work column.
> *"Print discipline, on screen."* Two phrases, set like a masthead.
> *"We design and build products for small ambitious teams."* Names the verbs and the audience.
