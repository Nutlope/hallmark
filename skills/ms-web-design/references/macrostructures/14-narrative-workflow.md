## 14 · Narrative Workflow

Numbered stages tell the story of how the user uses the product over time. Each section is a phase; the page is a process timeline.

- **Heading:** the stage number rides inside the heading (`<h2>1.0 Intake</h2>`) or is generated by `<ol>` with `counter()`. Never a separate label beside the heading: that is an eyebrow at stage scale (54).
- **Body:** each stage has a short explanation and one object at full size: a product capture 360px tall or more when the user gave screens, otherwise a figure or rows from the prompt (a timer, a count, the thing the stage makes). Three stages holding the same kind of object read as three features (8): vary the object from stage to stage.
- **Divider:** a rule between stages, in the theme's rule weight; the number stays in the heading, never on the rule.
- **Button:** the hero's one action (44b), returning once at the foot ("Start at stage one"); links inside stages.
- **Image:** a real capture per stage, often annotated, in a `<figure>` or a slot; with one screen or none, never a slot per stage (68).
- **Reveal:** one horizontal sweep, on the first stage, when the theme allows a reveal; the later stages are simply there. A scrub-linked stage sequence may use [`../scroll-choreography.md`](../scroll-choreography.md) § 1 Pinned chapter instead: one pattern per page.

Reach for it for products with explicit workflows: project management, design-to-dev pipelines, writing tools.

Avoid for tools that work in one moment. Narrative Workflow needs a real sequence.

Compositions: Steps, each stage with its capture (`layout.md` § 5).

**Sample opening lines** (stage headings: numbered, declarative, in process language, one stage per line):
> *"1. Sourdough overnight. 2. Score at dawn. 3. Pull at seven."* Three numbered stages, no marketing (Maple Street Bread).
> *"1.0 Parse. 2.0 Filter. 3.0 Route."* Versioned numbers, three concrete verbs (Streampipe).
> *"I. We design products that last twelve years. II. A material is sustainable when someone can repair it."* Roman numerals and declaratives (Meridian).
