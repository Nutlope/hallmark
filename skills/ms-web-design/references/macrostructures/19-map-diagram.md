## 19 · Map / Diagram

A single large spatial diagram organises the page: flowchart, floor plan, network graph, system map. Information is laid out spatially, not linearly.

- **Heading:** an orientation head at the section size above or beside the map; the map carries the page.
- **Body:** the map itself, nodes and edges, with a legend.
- **Divider:** none inside; the diagram is one composition.
- **Button:** links inside nodes; the page's one action sits with the orientation phrase, inside the fold (44b), never under the map.
- **Image:** the map is the image: SVG drawn from the real places or the real system, with hover states, labelled or `aria-hidden` with a text legend carrying every fact (33). Geometry that is geometry is a first-class medium (47c).
- **Reveal:** node-by-node ink-on as the map enters the viewport, capped at five nodes, once.

Reach for it for system overviews, ecosystem maps, process diagrams, organisation charts.

Avoid as a substitute for narrative. Some stories should not be read spatially.

Mobile: under 60rem the map scrolls sideways inside a wrapper, or collapses to an ordered list of its nodes.

**Sample opening lines** (name the path or the territory):
> *"Browser, then API, then database. Tracejam sits between the last two."* Names the layers and the position.
> *"From stdin, through the pipe, into your dashboard."* Labels the flow drawn below.
> *"Three tiers across two regions, one ledger."* Counts the system, geographic and structural.
