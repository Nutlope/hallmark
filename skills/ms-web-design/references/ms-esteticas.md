# ms · Visual territories

A vocabulary for `ms-arte.md`: nine territories a portfolio or a personal site tends to fall into, each translated into this skill's genres, themes and shapes. A territory is a starting point for a conversation about references, never a template, and never a theme by itself: the theme files still decide the material. Inside a client system, a territory only informs what the brand leaves open (`ms-arte.md` § The brand comes first).

The example sites behind these territories stay in the user's local reference folder, outside this repository.

## Monochrome

Black, white and the greys between, with type doing nearly all the work: a display voice, a plain reading face, small quiet metadata, strong hierarchy and very little ornament. Fits when the work and the writing should carry the site, often for senior or technical profiles. Risk: sterile, hard to scan, or a stock developer page. Personality comes from type, one detail or the pictures, never from adding decoration. Watch gate 64 when a mono face appears on labels without being the material.

## Personal space

A site that feels like the owner's corner of the internet: layered pictures, scans, notes in the owner's own hand, objects placed with deliberate imperfection, old-web or stationery references. Fits when personality is part of what is hired. Risk: the work gets lost and the page turns to noise. Everything drawn must be a real scan or photograph used as an image: a drawing made in SVG or CSS is gate 80. The collage is composed in CSS so it reads with scripting off; dragging is the layer.

## Quiet minimal

Reduction, space and calm: neutral ground, refined type, few interface elements, work shown like a product. Fits when the work already has weight and needs no staging. Risk: clean and forgettable, close to a startup default. Guard against the centred hero on one axis (gate 6b) and the stock template run (gate 8); the specificity has to live in the type and the work itself.

## Sidebar and window

A narrow persistent column holds identity, navigation and the list of work; a wide pane shows the selected piece. Fits a body of several pieces that a visitor browses while keeping their bearings. Risk: a crammed sidebar, or a main pane that turns into a card gallery. Without script each piece is its own page or an anchor target, and the sidebar is sticky in CSS.

## Code and design

The language of editors, terminals and technical tools: monospace where it measures something, structured metadata, coordinates, file-like labels. Fits people who really build. Risk: costume, meaning fake commands, meaningless syntax and green on black. The technical voice must describe how the owner actually works; mono must be the material for gate 64 to pass.

## Experimental

Unusual scale, cropping, layering, colour, texture or space: the site is partly a visual piece. Fits when art direction is the thing being hired. Risk: the visitor has to solve the interface before seeing the work. Always the custom route, `direction.md`, with the navigation toll in `ms-critica.md`.

## Motion first

Movement as a main part of the identity: transitions, scroll-linked type, image sequencing, consistent timing. Fits when interaction or motion craft is the work. Risk: exhausting, slow, and the content arrives late. Only through the posture in `ms-movimiento.md`: the static page is complete first, then movement goes on top.

## Editorial

The pacing of magazines and books: large heads over restrained reading text, captions, annotations, pages composed rather than stacked. Fits strong writing and long case studies. Risk: everything huge, or beauty that slows scanning. Long pages still have to scan quickly.

## Combination

Not a territory of its own: the method of `ms-arte.md` § 4, where one reference sets the system and the others each lend one ingredient under shared rules. It takes its genre, base and shape from the dominant reference.
